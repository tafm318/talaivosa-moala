I am a farmer on a 1.5-acre market garden. I can afford to plant 64 beds total of tomatoes, carrots, and mescluns with the help of 4 temporary workers. There is a bed caps sum of 70: 20 beds of tomatoes, 20 beds of carrots, and 30 beds of mescluns. I cannot max out on every crop, so I need to decide which crop must give. Other factors to consider are revenue per bed, field hours per week per bed, fertilizer per bed, and diminishing-returns rate. Below is a table highlighting the factors for each crop. 

| Crop | Bed Cap | Revenue/Bed | Field HRS/WK/BED | Fertilizer/Bed | Diminishing Returns Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | 
| Tomatoes | 20 beds | $8,800 | 2.5 | $880 | 10% |
| Carrots | 20 beds | $2,094 | 0.833 | $440 | 2.5% |
| Mescluns | 30 beds | $2,700 | 1.25 | $880 | 1.25% |

Tomatoes have the highest revenue per bed but the highest diminishing returns rate which means plant fewer but the maximum amount. I think 16 tomato beds will be okay. Carrots have a much lower diminishing returns rate than tomatoes but slightly higher than mescluns and it has the lowest cost for revenue per bed and fertilizer per bed. These numbers seem low enough to take a chance and max out and plant 20 carrot beds. Mescluns has the lowest diminishing returns rate but it's cost per bed is just as high as the tomatoes, which means it is expensive to plant but more can be planted. I think 28 mesclun beds will be okay. My hypothesis is I will profit the most by planting 16 tomato beds, 20 carrot beds, and 28 mesclun beds.       

I expect the tomato beds and mesclun beds to stop when their marginal cost catches up to their price. I expect the carrot beds to stop when it hits it bed cap.
